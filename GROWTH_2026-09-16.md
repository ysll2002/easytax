## 1. The report has been counting visitors and cannot see users

This is the finding of the round, and it inverts what the last three rounds
believed about this product.

On the evening of 2026-09-15 somebody arrived from Google, registered, and
spent about an hour moving through the dashboard: company, P&L, banking,
reconcile, profile, HMRC. The next morning they came back and did it again.
Their sessions produced 108 engaged events.

Across the whole of the last seven days:

| | |
|---|---|
| Dashboard events, humans, production | **192** |
| …of which `page_engaged` | **90** |
| Distinct signed-in accounts | 5 (most of them the owner's own test accounts) |
| Busiest areas | `/dashboard/individual` 52, `/dashboard` 47, `/dashboard/company` 44, `/dashboard/banking` 24 |

**None of that appears anywhere in the daily report.** `audience()` and
`funnel()` both filter through `isPublicContentPath`, which excludes
`/dashboard` — correctly, for their purpose, which is "did the marketing
work". The consequence was that a payload 1,100 lines long could tell you 19
people looked at a public page and could not tell you that anybody had used
the product.

So the 09-15 snapshot reported `human.visitors: 16`, `engagement.events: 11`,
and five rounds of strategy have been steered by a report that sees arrivals
and is blind to usage. F48.

I nearly made the opposite error writing this. The first read of
`engagement.avg_depth_pct: 7` looked like a catastrophic bounce rate. It is
not a bounce rate: `EngagementTracker` sends on the *first* signal, so depth
and dwell are lower bounds by construction — the component says so in its own
comments. What the data actually shows is 9 of 11 first signals being a click
or a keypress, which is the discrimination the tracker was built for, and it
says these are people rather than crawlers.

## 2. Every machine reading this site was reported as "Mozilla"

`distribution.feeds.by_agent` has said `[{ "key": "Mozilla", "count": 51 }]`
for as long as it has existed, and the round that shipped it read that as one
generic poller.

The keying rule was "cut the user-agent at the first space or slash". Nearly
every crawler on earth opens its UA with `Mozilla/5.0`, so the rule collapsed
all of them into one bucket and the report then presented that bucket as
though it were a product name.

The identification was never missing. `lib/bot-detection.ts` already resolves
these and stamps `bot_label` on the row at write time. `distribution()` simply
never read it. Keyed on the label that was already there:

| Surface | Old | Actual |
|---|---|---|
| Feeds | `Mozilla` ×51 | **gptbot 14, meta-externalagent 10, oai-searchbot 2, perplexitybot 1**, unidentified 35 |
| Calendar (`.ics`) | *not reported at all* | **gptbot 12, meta-externalagent 8**, unidentified 24 |
| Embeds | `Mozilla` | petalbot 1 |

Two things follow.

**The answer engines are reading this site every day.** OpenAI, Meta and
Perplexity are pulling the feeds and the calendar. `oai-searchbot` is the
notable one: that is OpenAI's *search* crawler rather than its training
crawler, which is the path to being cited in a ChatGPT answer.

**`calendar_fetched` was not in the payload at all.** 44 fetches in seven days
— the single largest machine signal on the site, against 95 public page views
— and every round that discussed it had to query Supabase by hand. F39 shipped
a calendar CTA in a round whose demand side was unreportable. F49.

This is the fifth instance of one failure: a control exists, and the number
that would show it misfiring does not. The review gate with no
`days_since_last_publish`. The deploy that could not say what was live. The SEO
audit with no caller. The crawl pointed at a login page. Now an agent breakdown
that named every client `Mozilla`.

## 3. The review queue was mostly the same page twice

## What shipped

Three of the five are traffic, which is short of the standing 80/20 split and
deliberate: two of this round's findings were that the instruments steering
the traffic work were lying, and a traffic feature built on a lying instrument
is the mistake five previous rounds made.

### F46 — Reconcile the queue before asking anyone to clear it
`lib/queue-reconcile.ts` · `/api/cron/daily`

Drafts whose headline is already claimed by a published article or a
hand-built landing page are moved to `rejected`, oldest-survives, before the
alarm email is composed — the email is built from the queue, so a pass that
ran after it would have mailed the same page twice.

It cannot publish and cannot delete. It only declines to ask a person about
work that duplicates something they already have, which changes nothing a
reader sees and is what makes it safe to run unattended where releasing a
draft is not.

- **Measures:** the `queue-reconcile` cron step; `editorial.drafts_awaiting_review`.
- **Baseline:** 3 drafts, of which 2 duplicates — a 67% busywork rate in the
  one queue a person has to want to open.
- **Target:** the reviewer is never shown a duplicate. Verified by dry run
  against the live queue: rejects exactly the two 09-11/09-13 drafts, keeps the
  VAT threshold one, and flags 0 false positives.

### F47 — An article states the question it was written to answer
`lib/article-question.ts` · `supabase/migrations/20260916_article_target_query.sql`
· `/api/cron/daily-article` · `app/tax-tips/[slug]`

Every article since 2026-09-08 has been commissioned against one specific
search, and F44 now fails the headline in quality review if it does not cover
that query. The query itself was then thrown away — never a column, never
rendered. The pipeline knew precisely which question each of 113 pages answers
and every one of them went out without saying it.

It is now stored on the row, rendered above the excerpt, and folded as the
first entry into the article's existing `FAQPage` node rather than emitted as
a second one. The answer is the article's own excerpt and never new text:
generating a fresh "short answer" would be unreviewed tax guidance reaching
the page through a side door.

**Honest scope: this changes 3 pages today.** Only 3 of 113 published articles
match a curated query under `coversQuery`, which is itself the finding — the
archive was written demand-blind and the anchor arrived at article 114. It
gains one page per article published from here.

- **Measures:** articles rendering a Q&A block; `target_query` non-null on new rows.
- **Baseline:** 0 of 113.
- **Target:** every new article, plus the 3 existing matches. Verified against
  a production build: exactly one `FAQPage` node, the commissioned question
  first, non-matching articles untouched.

### F48 — The report can see people using the product
`/api/admin/daily-metrics`

An `activation` block, counted on exactly the `/dashboard` paths the other
blocks exclude: signed-in people, how many reached the dashboard, engaged
events, and which areas of the product they went to.

Counted by `user_id` rather than `anon_id`, because an anon id changes between
a logged-out and a logged-in session — the 09-15 user appears under two, and
counting anon ids would have reported one person as two.

- **Measures:** `activation.last_7d.*`.
- **Baseline:** the payload had no field of any kind describing product use.
- **Target:** the 09-15 user is visible. Verified against live events: 192
  dashboard events, 90 engaged, 5 accounts, by area.

### F49 — Name the machines that are actually reading this
`/api/admin/daily-metrics`

`agentKey()` prefers the `bot_label` already stamped at write time, falls back
to the UA product token, and never emits a browser marker as though it were a
client — `Mozilla` alone identifies nothing and now reads as the unknown it
is. `calendar_fetched` joins the block.

- **Measures:** `distribution.feeds.by_agent`, `distribution.calendar.*`.
- **Baseline:** one bucket, `Mozilla`, 111 events across every surface.
- **Target:** named clients. Verified against 400 live rows: gptbot,
  meta-externalagent, oai-searchbot, perplexitybot, petalbot resolved; the
  genuinely unknown remainder labelled `unidentified-client` rather than
  given a false name.

### F50 — Tell the people who signed up where they stand
`components/FilingStatus.tsx` · `/api/filing-waitlist` · `app/dashboard`

48 accounts, 17 with HMRC connected, 0 filings — because filing needs HMRC
production access and that review is still open. No screen in the product said
so. Everyone who has ever signed up found they could not do the thing they came
for and left, and `launch_subscribers` — the list to mail on approval day — has
zero rows in it.

A banner above the activation checklist, deliberately above rather than below:
the checklist asks the user to do things, and putting the ask first and the
caveat underneath is how people end up feeling misled by a product that was
only ever early. It names no date, because we do not have one. One tap
subscribes, on the address the account already has — the route accepts no email
in its body, so it cannot be used to add arbitrary addresses to a mailing list.

- **Measures:** `launch_list.total`; `launch_subscribed` with `source: dashboard`.
- **Baseline:** 0 subscribers, ever, from 5 public placements in 13 days.
- **Target:** > 0 within 7 days. With ~1 new registration a week this is a low
  bar deliberately; the value is that the 48 already registered now have
  somewhere to say yes.

## Deploy notes

`npx tsc --noEmit` and `npm run build` pass. Article rendering, the FAQPage
merge and the agent keying were each verified against real data rather than
reasoned about — the rendering against a local production build, the keying
and the reconcile against live Supabase rows.

One defect was found and fixed during that verification: `asQuestion()`
rendered the query's lowercase pronoun literally, producing *"Can i appeal a
HMRC late filing penalty"* in a heading and in the `FAQPage` name a search
engine may quote back.

`/dashboard` returns 500 on a local production server. That is pre-existing and
not from this branch: auth.js rejects `127.0.0.1` as an untrusted host, `auth()`
returns null, and `app/dashboard/layout.tsx:30` reads `user.image` off it. The
line is identical on `main`.

Nothing here touches `lib/hmrc*.ts`, `app/api/hmrc/**`, `auth.ts`,
`middleware.ts`, `DeviceDataCollector.tsx` or `app/api/auth/callback/hmrc/`.

**`supabase/migrations/20260916_article_target_query.sql` needs to be run.**
Until it is, `insertDraft` retries without the column and warns — deliberately
*before* the pre-review-gate fallback, so that a missing cosmetic column can
never drop an article into the path that publishes without review.

Still outstanding, none of them mine to decide:

- **Three drafts are waiting, the oldest nine days old.** After this deploys,
  one of them is a real decision and two are gone. It is one click at
  `/admin/review`, and the archive is still the only part of this site search
  has ever sent anyone to.
- **The reactivation campaign is built and unsent**, six rounds running. It
  mails 45 real people who have never received anything from us. Outward-facing
  and irreversible, and still the strongest revenue lever before approval.

not see them and not that we could not reach them. It is that what we are
building is not yet worth waiting for, and the next round should be about the
product rather than about its measurement or its marketing.
