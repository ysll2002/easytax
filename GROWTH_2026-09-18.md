## 1. The pipeline died on a quotation mark

This is the finding of the round.

The 2026-09-17 editorial run recorded:

```
mode: "none"   written: 0
error: "Expected ',' or '}' after property value in JSON at position 6196
        (line 4 column 5970)"
```

Line 4 is `content`. Position 6196 is five thousand characters into a
2,500-word HTML body. The model emitted **one `"` inside the string without
escaping it**. The parser closed the string there, found prose where it wanted
a comma, and threw.

What made that cost a day rather than a retry is where the throw went.
`parseDraft` was called from `callModel` with nothing between it and the
handler's outer `catch`, so it propagated *past* the quality retry, *past* the
rest of the batch, and out of the run. The archive's only engine for adding
pages produced nothing, and the sole record was an error string in a snapshot
row nobody reads.

Three things were wrong and all three are fixed (F56):

- there was no recovery for a response that is *nearly* JSON;
- there was no regeneration when the response could not be read at all — the
  existing retry grades *quality*, and a parse error happens before anything is
  graded;
- one assignment's failure discarded every other assignment in the batch.

## 2. Two thirds of the archive is cut off in the search result

`seo.problem_counts.title_over_60` has read **80** for days. Of 114 published
articles, 80 render a `<title>` longer than the 60 characters Google displays —
median 63, longest 78. On more than two thirds of the only pages search can
send anyone to, the headline a searcher reads is truncated mid-phrase at the
one moment they decide whether to click.

This is a debt, not a leak. `STANDARD.maxTitleChars` has capped new headlines
at the budget since 2026-09-14; all 80 predate it. And `buildTitle` was not
wrong to let them through — its Rule 3 says "leave it whole rather than mangle
it, and let the audit say so", which was correct while there was no safe
shortening. There is one now (F57).

The first thing tried was cutting at the colon, and it is worth recording why
it was thrown away: *"Adjusted Profit Calculations: Mistakes That Cost
Directors Thousands"* becomes *"Adjusted Profit Calculations"* — 28 characters
that discard the entire reason to click. It would have flattened half the
archive to a bare subject.

## What shipped

### F56 — One unescaped quotation mark no longer costs the archive a day
`lib/draft-json.ts` · `/api/cron/daily-article`

Three defences, in order of preference.

`parseDraft` tries a strict parse, then one **content-preserving** repair for
the failure actually observed: it locates the known keys and reads each value
between its opening quote and the quote that closes it. A stray `"` in the
middle is then simply part of the value, which is what the model meant. The
repair does not guess — it declines rather than returning a partial draft, and
declining is what a truncated response gets.

Deciding *which* quote closes a value is the whole problem, and the obvious
rule is wrong. "The first quote followed by a comma" silently truncates a body
containing `He said "go", then left`. A terminator must be followed by
something only a terminator can be followed by: a comma and the opening of
another known key, or a closing brace and the end of the object. That case is
in the tests because the first implementation failed it.

When the repair declines, the model is asked again with an instruction about
escaping — a second sample is a better bet than a cleverer parser and cannot
corrupt anything. Only a parse error is retried; an API error or a rate limit
is not something a reworded prompt fixes.

And a failure is now **this assignment's outcome**, not the run's. The loop
continues, the trace is still written, and a `?count=5` run no longer discards
four untried assignments because the first one stumbled.

- **Measures:** `editorial.last_run.{error, written, outcomes}`; articles
  written per week.
- **Baseline:** 2026-09-17 — `mode: none`, `written: 0`, one JSON error, whole
  run lost.
- **Target:** no run ever records zero written *because of* a parse failure.
  Verified by 16 tests covering the exact 09-17 failure shape, escaped quotes,
  reordered keys, truncated responses, and the `"go", then left` case.

### F57 — The search result shows the whole headline
`lib/serp-title.ts` · `lib/seo-meta.ts`

A short list of reductions that remove words carrying no search intent — "How
to", "Your", "Explained", a trailing "— A Practical Guide", `and` → `&`.
Candidates are built cumulatively and the one chosen is the **longest that
fits**, not the first, which is what stops it over-shortening: "Vehicle Finance
vs Operating Leases: Tax Rules for Sole Traders" needs to lose two characters,
and a rule set applied in order until something fits hands back 46.

**The H1 is untouched.** So are `og:title` and the JSON-LD `headline`. This
produces the `<title>` tag only — a display string — and the page a reader
lands on still carries the headline as written. Verified on rendered pages:
`<title>` 59 characters, `<h1>` 66, same URL.

One rule was written, measured and removed: `When Does … Count` →
`When … Count` saved five characters and produced a headline that is not
English. No rule here may change a verb's form. A title this cannot shorten is
left alone, which is always an acceptable result — 43 of them are.

- **Measures:** `seo.problem_counts.title_over_60`; `seo.median_title_length`;
  search-referred visitors per week.
- **Baseline:** 80 of 114 over budget, median title 63.
- **Target:** ≤ 45 over budget, median ≤ 60. **Measured on a production build
  of this branch: 80 → 43, median 63 → 59.** All 37 changed titles were read
  individually for grammar.

### F58 — The snippet ends on a finished thought
`lib/seo-meta.ts` · 13 page files

`metaDescription` gains a clause-boundary fallback for the case where no
sentence ends inside the budget: a dash, semicolon or colon ends a clause
cleanly, and a late comma will do. Mid-phrase truncations fall from 19 to 8.

The 13 hand-written page descriptions were **rewritten, not truncated**.
Machine-truncating them was tried first and rejected: it took
`/mtd-software` from 231 characters to 104 by dropping the sentence that
contains the price, which trades an invisible tail for a snippet that no longer
says what the product costs. These are commercial pages, not regulated
guidance, so rewriting them needs no review gate. Every one now states the
price or the promise inside 160 characters.

- **Measures:** `seo.problem_counts.description_outside_70_160`; articles whose
  description ends in an ellipsis.
- **Baseline:** 13 pages outside the range; 19 articles ending mid-phrase.
- **Target:** 0 and ≤ 10. **Measured: 13 → 0, and 19 → 8.**

### F59 — Put the facts where the fetches are
`app/tax-tips/_lib/feed.ts` · `feed.xml` · `feed.json`

The **Key facts** block now leads both feeds' channel description — 69 fetches
a week instead of one. It is computed per request from `lib/mtd-dates.ts` and
`lib/mtd-status.ts`, the same functions the timetable and the deadline checker
render from, so the feed cannot state a threshold the site has since corrected.
Being cited accurately is the point; being cited saying something we have since
fixed everywhere else would be worse than not being cited.

Items gain a **lead section** — the excerpt plus the opening of the body, cut
at an element boundary — as `content:encoded` in RSS and `content_html` in the
JSON feed, where `content_text` previously repeated the excerpt verbatim.

This is deliberately not the full body. The JSON feed shipped with a comment
explaining why the body stays home: "syndicating the full body would hand a
scraper a clean copy of 113 pages we are trying to rank ourselves." That
concern is real and this does not dismiss it — an answer engine judging whether
a page answers a question cannot do it from two sentences, and a scraper
reproducing the archive needs all of it. `LEAD_CHARS` is one constant if the
owner decides the citation is worth more than the copy.

- **Measures:** `distribution.feeds.by_agent`; referrals from `chatgpt.com`,
  `perplexity.ai` and `notebook.google.com`, which `channelOf` already breaks
  out by host.
- **Baseline:** 69 feed fetches/week carrying headlines only; 6 search-referred
  visitors; 3 referrals from `notebook.google.com` all-time.
- **Target:** feed fetches hold or rise, and ≥ 1 answer-engine referral in 30
  days. Verified against a production build: RSS parses as valid XML under a
  real parser, 50 of 50 items carry `content:encoded`, the `content` namespace
  is declared, no lead is cut mid-tag, and no lead contains a raw `]]>`.

### F60 — The report names the defects it counts
`lib/seo-audit.ts` · `/api/admin/daily-metrics`

`worst_offenders` listed five categories and **every one of them was empty**,
while the three that were not — 80 titles, 13 descriptions, 7 thin pages — were
bare integers with no way to learn which pages they were. The report named only
the problems it did not have. Acting on "80" required running your own crawl,
which is what this round did and what the next one should not have to.

It now names `title_over_60`, `description_outside_70_160`,
`thin_under_600_words` and `orphan_risk_few_internal_links`, worst-first and
capped at ten each. It found the 13 over-long descriptions within minutes of
being written; F58 fixed them the same afternoon.

`jsonLdTypes` now walks `@graph` — see §5 — so PR #21 lands without appearing
to delete the site's structured data.

And `editorial.archive_depth` reports what no field has ever carried: 114
published, 573–724 words, median 641, **0 meeting the 1,100-word standard**,
and 228 days to clear at one rewrite every other day.

- **Measures:** `seo.worst_offenders`; `editorial.archive_depth`.
- **Baseline:** four non-zero problem categories, none of them nameable from
  the payload; no field describing the archive's depth.
- **Target:** every non-zero category names its worst pages.
  **Verified on a production build over 129 URLs.**

## Deploy notes

`npx tsc --noEmit` and `npm run build` pass. Nothing here was reasoned about
without being measured: the titles and descriptions against a 129-URL crawl of
a production build, the feeds against a real XML parser, the JSON repair
against 16 tests, and every "before" number against live Supabase rows.

Two things found during verification and deliberately **not** changed:

- **Three of the 114 stored articles contain a stray `</li>`** — unbalanced in
  the database, so the live page already renders it and the feed lead
  faithfully reproduces it. Fixing stored content is an editorial change, not a
  rendering one.
- **8 pages report `canonical_mismatch` in a local crawl of every published
  slug.** That is F51 working: they are the near-duplicates pointing at their
  cluster primary. Production crawls `sitemap.xml`, which `withoutDuplicates`
  already excludes them from, so this will not appear in tomorrow's report.

Nothing here touches `lib/hmrc*.ts`, `app/api/hmrc/**`, `auth.ts`,
`middleware.ts`, `DeviceDataCollector.tsx` or `app/api/auth/callback/hmrc/`.

Still outstanding, none of them mine to decide:

- **`supabase/migrations/20260916_article_target_query.sql` has still not
  run.** Confirmed directly: `tax_articles` has no `target_query` column, two
  days after F47 shipped against it. F53 (in PR #22, merged here) reports it as
  `schema.pending`. Everything downstream is fail-soft, so nothing is broken —
  F47 simply does nothing until the migration is applied.
- **One draft has been waiting 217 hours and the archive has published nothing
  for 10 days.** The freeze alarm is working and fires daily; the queue is one
  click at `/admin/review`. The archive is still the only part of this site
  search has ever sent anyone to.
- **The reactivation campaign is built and unsent**, seven rounds running. It
  mails 45 real people who have never received anything from us. Outward-facing
  and irreversible, and still the strongest revenue lever before approval.
- **The archive meets none of its own standard.** Batch the rewrites, or prune
  the weakest pages. See `editorial.archive_depth`.
