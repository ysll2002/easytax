# Growth work — 2026-09-08

Five changes against the three standing goals: traffic (the priority), trust,
and revenue. What shipped, what measures it, and how to judge it.

The finding that reorganised this round is not in the analytics.

---

## The site is five months out of date

MTD ITSA commenced on **6 April 2026**. Today is 8 September 2026. The £50,000
cohort — roughly 780,000 sole traders and landlords, on HMRC's own estimate —
has been inside the regime for five months and has already had one quarterly
update fall due, on 7 August. The next is due **7 November 2026**. And from
**September 2026 — this month** — HMRC has begun automatically signing up
people whose 2024/25 return showed qualifying income over £50,000 and who had
not signed up themselves.

Almost the entire site describes this as something that is going to happen.

| Page | What it said |
|---|---|
| `/timetable` | "from April 2026, you'll need to keep digital records" |
| `/bokio-alternative` | "When April 2026 hits, your first quarterly window opens" |
| `/bokio-alternative` | badge: "MTD ITSA ready for April 2026" |
| `/self-assessment-software` | "For now, you can still file directly on HMRC's website" |
| `/mtd-software` | "From April 2026, MTD-compatible software is required by law" |
| `/crunch-alternative` | "once HMRC MTD ITSA becomes mandatory" |

The homepage was the exception: it already computed its announcement from
`lib/mtd-dates` and said "Making Tax Digital has started." Nothing else did.

This matters three separate ways, which is why it took over the round:

1. **It is wrong.** "For now, you can still file directly on HMRC's website"
   is not stale phrasing, it is incorrect advice given to exactly the people
   we are selling to. Someone over the threshold who acts on that sentence
   misses a statutory deadline.
2. **It reads as abandoned.** A tax product that has not noticed a mandate
   commencing is one a visitor assumes has been left to rot. That is a trust
   problem no amount of `/trust` page copy fixes.
3. **It misses the demand.** The queries moved in April and again this month —
   from "when does MTD start" to "I missed my quarterly update", "HMRC signed
   me up for Making Tax Digital", "what counts as qualifying income". Content
   in the wrong tense does not match any of them.

The root cause is the same one the 2026-09-06 round found in the footers: the
claim was retyped by hand on twenty pages instead of derived once. So the fix
is not "correct the copy" — it is to stop writing the tense by hand, which is
what F11 and F12 do.

## What shipped

### F11 — The mandate's status, derived instead of retyped
`lib/mtd-status.ts` · 6 landing pages · `/timetable`

`getMtdStatus(now)` resolves, from the real dates in `lib/mtd-dates` and the
clock: whether the mandate is live, whether HMRC's auto-enrolment has begun,
which quarter is open, which one is due next, and how many days remain. Every
sentence about where we are in the regime now comes from it.

The nine hand-written claims above are corrected, and the two genuinely wrong
ones rewritten rather than re-tensed. "Can I still file on HMRC's website?" now
answers accurately: yes for your 2025/26 return, due 31 January 2027 — no for
2026/27 if you were over £50,000.

`/timetable` also learned what day it is. Each of its nine dates carries an ISO
date; passed ones are dimmed and marked **Passed**, the next carries a **Next
up** badge. It revalidates hourly, so it cannot freeze at deploy time. Before
this, the page opened with three deadlines in the past and said nothing about
it.

Verified across the timeline (2026-03-01 → 2027-05-08): phase, open quarter,
due quarter and day count are correct at every boundary, including the 7 August
roll-over from Q1-due to Q2-due.

- **Measures:** zero future-tense claims about a commenced rule (grep-checkable);
  `/timetable` and `/mtd-software` page views; `trust_viewed`.
- **Baseline:** 9 stale claims, 2 of them wrong advice. 1 view on `/timetable`.
- **Target:** 0 stale claims — already true and verified, not forecast.

### F12 — The live deadline bar, on every page
`components/MtdStatusBar.tsx` · `components/SiteHeader.tsx` · 7 locales

The homepage's announcement bar, generalised into the shared header: "MTD ITSA
is live. Q2 update (6 Jul – 5 Oct 2026) due 7 November 2026 · 60 days left",
with a link to the deadline checker. Hidden on `/` (which keeps its own richer
bar), on auth pages and on the dashboard.

It also solves a routing problem. `/mtd-deadline-checker` had **0 views in the
entire window**, despite being the single most useful thing we can give a
stranger before HMRC approval lands. It was reachable from the tools hub — which
also had 0 views — and the footer. It now has an inbound link from every public
page, above the fold.

The dated sentence renders server-side so crawlers and no-JS visitors see it;
the day count is filled in after mount from the reader's own clock, so a stale
deploy can never put a wrong countdown on the screen.

- **Measures:** `activation_cta_click` with `placement: "mtd_status_bar"`;
  `/mtd-deadline-checker` page views; `checker_started` / `checker_completed`.
- **Baseline:** 0 views, 0 starts on the checker.
- **Target:** ≥ 15 checker page views and ≥ 3 `checker_started` in 30 days.

### F13 — The content pipeline, anchored to demand
`lib/search-queries.ts` · `app/api/cron/daily-article/route.ts`

The daily cron asked a model to "suggest ONE specific, practical article topic"
and wrote whatever came back. That produced 113 articles and two search visits
a week. The titles are good tax writing — "Goodwill amortisation: why it's not
tax deductible in the UK" — and nobody types anything like them into Google.
Nothing in the pipeline was anchored to demand.

`lib/search-queries.ts` is that anchor: **41 queries as a worried person would
type them**, each mapped to the page on this site that should get the visit.
The cron now picks the highest-priority query the archive does not already
answer, and the prompt leads with the query rather than a topic.

The excerpt is required to *answer the question in 40 words or fewer*. That is
not cosmetic: the excerpt renders as the lead paragraph under the H1 and is
used verbatim as the meta description, so an answer-first excerpt is the
difference between a search result worth clicking and a teaser. It also makes
the page eligible for a featured snippet, which is the only way a domain with
our authority appears above an incumbent.

Two other changes. The prompt is now given the live mandate status from F11 and
told to write in the correct tense — the seed topic list literally said "what
self-employed people need to do **before** April 2026". And every article gains
an internal link to its mapped page, constructed server-side from our own
values rather than requested from the model, because the body is injected with
`dangerouslySetInnerHTML` and a model-authored `<a href>` there is an XSS
vector.

Coverage against the real 113-article archive: **3 of 41 answered, 38 pending**
— five weeks of queued work, highest-priority first. The first six are all
about the mandate people are inside right now.

The matcher took two passes. Plain word-overlap at 70% credited "Overlap
relief: claim tax relief when going self-employed" with answering "what can I
claim as expenses self-employed" — three words shared, and the one that
mattered missing. Worse, one generic title, "Making Tax Digital for Income Tax:
your pre-April 2026 checklist", cleared the bar for four unrelated MTD queries
at once and would have suppressed a month of the highest-priority work on the
strength of one article that is itself now out of date. It now also requires
every *distinctive* word of the query — rarest by document frequency across the
list — to be present. The three matches that survive are all genuine.

- **Measures:** `query_coverage` in the cron response; search-referred visitors
  in `funnel.attribution.*.by_channel`; page views on `/tax-tips/*`.
- **Baseline:** 3/41 covered. 2 search visitors/week. 113 articles, 3 of which
  earned a visit in the window.
- **Target:** ≥ 30/41 covered by 2026-10-08 (one a day); search-referred
  visitors ≥ 10/week by 2026-10-08.

### F14 — A calendar people can keep
`lib/tax-calendar.ts` · `/calendar/uk-tax-deadlines.ics` · `components/CalendarSubscribe.tsx`

The site has 130 pages and two organic visits a week. Page 131 repeats the
mistake. What it does not have is anything a person would *send to someone
else*, or keep.

A subscribable calendar is both. `/calendar/uk-tax-deadlines.ics` is a live
`.ics` feed covering three years of MTD quarterly updates, final declarations,
Self Assessment filing dates and payments on account — 21 events — with
reminders one week and one day before each. Every date comes from
`lib/mtd-dates`, so it cannot disagree with the rest of the site. No account, no
email address, nothing to approve: it works today, while HMRC approval is
pending, which is true of almost nothing else we own.

Deliberately not under `/api` — `robots.ts` disallows that prefix and this is a
public document we want crawled and linked. Promoted on `/timetable`,
`/mtd-deadline-checker` and in the site footer, with both a `webcal://`
subscribe link and a plain download.

Validated: 21 events, RFC 5545 line folding at 75 **octets** (counted in UTF-8
bytes and never splitting a multi-byte sequence — the descriptions contain £
signs and en dashes, and a naive character fold corrupts them in strict
clients), CRLF throughout, balanced VEVENT blocks.

- **Measures:** `calendar_fetched` (server-side, per fetch — a subscription
  shows up as a repeating request from a calendar user-agent);
  `calendar_cta_click` by `placement` and `mode`.
- **Baseline:** 0 — the asset did not exist.
- **Target:** ≥ 10 distinct calendar user-agents fetching the feed within 30
  days. A repeat fetch from Google/Apple/Outlook is the signal that someone
  actually subscribed rather than glanced.

### F15 — Stop counting our own crawlers as visitors
`app/api/admin/daily-metrics/route.ts` · `components/PageViewTracker.tsx`

Two fixes. At source, the tracker no longer fires on a path whose last segment
contains a dot — a request for a file that does not exist renders the 404 page,
which carries the tracker, so crawlers probing for key files and `robots.txt`
were recording themselves as visitors on paths no page ever lived at.

In the endpoint, `isPublicContentPath()` excludes static files, `/dashboard`,
`/api`, `/onboarding`, `/payment` and `/actions` from `by_path`, `by_channel`,
`unique_visitors` and every window total. The exclusions are **reported**, in a
new `excluded_non_content` block with the paths and counts — a filter you cannot
see is how a metric starts lying in the other direction.

Expect the headline visitor number to *drop* on the next run. That is the point.
The old number was not traffic.

- **Measures:** `excluded_non_content.page_views` / `.unique_visitors` against
  the reported totals.
- **Baseline:** 34 visitors reported, ~8 of them not people.
- **Target:** the two numbers reconcile; `excluded_non_content` trends toward 0
  as the source-side fix stops new rows being written.

| # | Metric | Baseline | 7-day | 30-day |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| F11 | stale future-tense mandate claims | 9 | 0 | 0 |
| F12 | `/mtd-deadline-checker` page views | 0 | ≥ 5 | ≥ 15 |
| F13 | target queries answered | 3/41 | ≥ 10/41 | ≥ 30/41 |
| F13 | search-referred visitors / week | 2 | ≥ 4 | ≥ 10 |
| F14 | distinct calendar user-agents | 0 | ≥ 3 | ≥ 10 |
| F15 | reported visitors reconcile with exclusions | — | yes | yes |

**F11 and F15 are already true** — they were verified on this commit, not
forecast. F12 and F14 are behavioural and readable in a week. F13 is the one
that matters and the one that cannot be judged before 30 days: an article
published today is not indexed today, and the archive has to accumulate before
the anchoring shows up in `by_channel`.

The number that would falsify this round: if 30 of 41 queries are answered on
2026-10-08 and search-referred visitors are still ~2/week, then the archive is
not the constraint and no amount of on-site work will make it one. The next
round would then have to be about getting linked from somewhere else — which is
the one thing none of the last four rounds has touched, because it is the one
thing that cannot be done from inside this repository.

## Not done this round, deliberately

**45 signups, 15 HMRC connections, 0 emails ever sent to them.** That is the
warmest audience this product has and it has never been contacted. It is the
strongest revenue lever available right now and it is not in this round for one
reason: it means sending mail to 45 real people, which is an outward-facing,
irreversible action that should be your call and not an autonomous agent's.
Proposed for the next round as a build-but-do-not-send: the campaign, the
opt-out handling (`lib/email-optout.ts` already exists) and a dry-run endpoint,
with the actual send behind an explicit manual trigger.
