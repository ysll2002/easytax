# Growth work — 2026-09-13

Five changes against the three standing goals: traffic (the priority), trust
and revenue. What shipped, what measures it, and how to judge it.

The finding that shaped this round is one number, and it is not a good one.

---

## 1. Seven human page views in two days, and every one of them was on `/`

Bot labelling (F24) went live in production at 2026-09-11T07:16. Of the 36
production events carrying a verdict since:

| | |
|---|---|
| Labelled production events | 36 |
| Automation | **29 (81%)** |
| Human | **7** |
| Human page views **not** on `/` | **0** |
| Human page views with any referrer | 1 (a Trustpilot link) |
| Search-referred visits, ever | 12 |
| Search-referred visits since 2026-09-09 | **0 — four days** |

Five of the seven arrived with no referrer at all, on a domain nobody has
heard of. Not one reached an article, a tool, or a topic hub.

Two stories fit that data exactly, and they imply opposite work:

1. Three or four people a day arrive, find nothing worth clicking, and leave.
   The problem is what is on the homepage.
2. Three or four automated clients a day arrive **with a browser User-Agent**,
   render the page, and leave — because that is all they ever do. The problem
   is that this site has essentially no human traffic, and four consecutive
   rounds have been tuning a page almost nobody has seen.

Every number this project currently has is identical under both readings. The
2026-09-11 round's closing line — "the difference between five and fifty will
not be made on the site itself" — assumed the first. It has never been tested,
and it decides where the next month goes. **F35 is the test.**

## 2. 149 of 185 production events still carry no verdict at all

The bot filter was wired into `/api/track`, which handles client events. The
four events recorded *server-side* — the `.ics` calendar, the RSS/JSON feeds,
the embed, `/llms.txt` — each stored the raw User-Agent string and nothing
else. So 43 of the recent rows, and 149 counting everything written before
2026-09-11, are unclassified.

That is worst precisely where those events live. The distribution numbers exist
to show a channel where *being read by crawlers is the good outcome*, and they
cannot show it while the rows are unclassified. Fixed in F35.

## 3. The pipeline has been competing with itself

`coverage()` computed which of the 41 target queries the site answers by
looking at `tax_articles` titles — and only those. So:

- `/hmrc-signed-me-up-for-mtd`, built on 2026-09-11 specifically to answer the
  single highest-priority query on the list, did not count. The cron went on
  treating that query as uncovered and **drafted an article for it anyway** —
  the 11,718-character draft still sitting in the review queue.
- The two calculators have answered four more priority-1 queries since the day
  each shipped, in their FAQs, with FAQPage markup, on pages carrying sitemap
  priority 0.9. None counted either.

Two pages on this domain targeting one long-tail query is not twice the chance
of ranking. It is the same chance, split, plus a day of the pipeline spent not
answering anything new. Fixed in F34.

### F31 — The review email becomes a button, and a freeze sets off an alarm
`lib/email.ts` · `app/api/cron/daily-article/route.ts`

The 2026-09-12 round found the archive frozen for five days and built
`/admin/review` to unfreeze it. It is still frozen: **the last article a reader
could see went up on 2026-09-07, six days ago**, and two drafts are waiting.
A page that can release them does not release them on its own.

The daily email was doing two things wrong, and both are why nobody ever acted
on it. Its call to action was
`GET /api/admin/article-review?key=<AGENT_METRICS_KEY>` — open a terminal, find
the secret, write a curl command with a JSON body. And it fired *only on days
the cron wrote something*, so a queue that stopped being added to stopped being
mentioned, which is exactly backwards: the longer nothing publishes, the
quieter it got.

Now: every draft is a link straight into `/admin/review` with its quality
report, the send decision is made by `reviewEmailIsDue()` from the queue's
state rather than from the run's output, and a freeze of two days or more puts
itself **in the subject line**. "2 drafts awaiting review" was true for five
days and told nobody anything was wrong.

The admin key travels in the link. That is a secret in an inbox and a
deliberate trade: the alternative is the status quo, where the control is
technically available and practically unreachable. The key opens only the
editorial queue, and it is rotatable. The email says so.

- **Measures:** `editorial.days_since_last_publish`; drafts released per week;
  the cron's new `review_notification` block.
- **Baseline:** 6 days frozen, 2 drafts waiting, 0 ever released through the UI.
- **Target:** `days_since_last_publish` ≤ 1 within 7 days, and the review queue
  empty at least once. Verified: 5/5 cases of the send decision
  (new drafts / empty queue / 1 day stale / frozen / unknown).

### F32 — The MTD question where the popular answer is wrong
`app/mtd-quarterly-update-deadlines/page.tsx` · `lib/mtd-penalties.ts` · sitemap · footer · 7 locales

Four priority-1 queries, none answered anywhere on the site:

- *i missed my mtd quarterly update deadline what happens*
- *making tax digital penalty points how do they work*
- *making tax digital income tax deadlines 2026 27*
- *what do i put in an mtd quarterly update*

This is worth a first-class route rather than article 116 because **the widely
published answer to the first one is currently wrong**. Search it and you are
told about penalty points and a £200 charge. For a late *quarterly update* in
2026/27 neither applies: HMRC is running a first-year soft landing and is not
charging late submission penalties for them. The person typing that at midnight
has just missed the 7 August or is about to miss the 7 November, and has been
told by four other sites that they owe something they do not.

Being a correction rather than a restatement is the whole opportunity, and it
is the kind of thing an answer engine quotes.

Three distinctions do the work, and dropping any of them is how the wrong
answer spreads. The concession covers quarterly updates, **not** the tax
return. It says nothing about **paying** late, which is charged from the start.
And it **expires** on 6 April 2027.

So the rules are data in `lib/mtd-penalties.ts`, keyed by tax year, and the page
reads them through `penaltyRegimeFor()`. A hand-typed "there is no penalty"
would be correct for seven months and then become the wrong answer it was
written to correct — which is not hypothetical, the homepage's hardcoded
"5 Aug 2026" did exactly that. Verified across 2026/27, 2027/28 and 2028/29:
the soft landing switches itself off and the percentages step up on their own.

Every fact was checked against four independent sources (GOV.UK's penalties and
quarterly-updates guidance, LITRG, ICAEW) rather than written from memory. I
had the first-year position wrong before checking, which is precisely why this
page is worth having.

- **Measures:** page views; search-referred visits landing on it
  (`attribution.by_path`); `topic_hub_viewed` with
  `topic: mtd_quarterly_deadlines`; `launch_subscribed` with
  `source: quarterly_deadlines`.
- **Baseline:** 0 — the page did not exist, and nothing on the site answered
  any of the four queries.
- **Target:** ≥ 40 page views and ≥ 5 search-referred visits by 2026-10-13.
  Judged at 30 days, not 7: a page published today is not indexed today.

### F33 — Questions that are marked up as questions
`components/FaqSection.tsx` · `components/ShortAnswer.tsx` · the landing pages

Two shared components, and one correction to what I thought I was fixing.

`FaqSection` renders an FAQ **and** emits its FAQPage JSON-LD from the same
array. Google's policy requires the marked-up answer to be visible on the page;
keeping both as one expression makes that true by construction. I had recorded
that `/hmrc-signed-me-up-for-mtd` emitted no schema at all — it did, built by
hand at the top of the file and rendered by a second hand-rolled block further
down. Adding the component produced **two FAQPage blocks on one URL**, which is
worse than none. The duplicate is gone, the hand-rolled pair with it, and every
public page now emits exactly one — verified across nine routes.

`ShortAnswer` is the other half. Every question-targeting page on this site
opened by describing what the page would do for the reader — "Enter three
things and you will see exactly which bands you are in". That is a fine second
paragraph and a bad first one: a featured snippet is built from a passage that
answers the query, and an answer engine lifts whatever the page says first.

- **Measures:** FAQPage blocks per URL (exactly 1, never 2); search-referred
  visits to the four pages carrying a short answer.
- **Baseline:** 1 duplicate FAQPage shipped and removed in the same round; 0
  pages leading with a direct answer.
- **Target:** ≥ 1 page showing a rich result or being cited by an answer engine
  by 2026-10-13. Weak as targets go, and honest about it: we have no Search
  Console and cannot observe a SERP from here.

### F34 — The site already answered more than it knew
`lib/search-queries.ts` · the two calculators · `lib/llms.ts`

`LANDING_PAGE_ANSWERS` registers the queries answered by a hand-built route, and
`coverage()` now counts the site rather than the archive. Ten queries move from
"pending" to "covered" without a word being written, because they were already
answered — see §3. The cron stops drafting duplicates of pages that exist and
rank better than a new article would.

A route earns an entry only if it answers the query directly and is indexable.
Keyed by the exact query string, so editing a query without updating the map
shows up as uncovered again — the safe direction, since a stale entry would
hide it.

Alongside: both calculators lead with the answer now, and the penalty
calculator finally answers *hmrc late payment interest rate 2026* — a
priority-1 query nothing on this site addressed. The mechanism is stated (base
rate plus 4 percentage points, running from the due date, not appealable) and
the current percentage is deliberately left to HMRC. A number typed here would
be wrong within one Bank Rate decision, and a stale rate on a tax site is worse
than no rate.

Both landing pages are also now in `/llms.txt`'s key-pages list, where neither
was, with the 2026/27 concession spelled out in the note — the AI crawlers are
the one channel with a pulse.

- **Measures:** `editorial.query_coverage` in daily-metrics, split into
  `covered_by_landing_page`; the cron's `next_up` never naming a query a live
  page already answers.
- **Baseline:** 3 of 41 covered, 38 pending, and the #1 query being drafted for
  the second time.
- **Target:** ≥ 15 of 41 covered by 2026-10-13, with zero duplicate drafts.

### F35 — Is anybody actually reading? (measurement)
`components/EngagementTracker.tsx` · `lib/bot-detection.ts` · `/api/track` · daily-metrics

The test for §1. A crawler that executes our JavaScript fires `page_view`
identically to a reader. It does not scroll half way down a page and sit there
for fifteen seconds. `page_engaged` records depth, seconds and whether anything
was clicked or typed — at most once per page view, with passive listeners and
no layout reads in the handler.

It reports **what happened, not a verdict**: depth, dwell and three booleans
rather than a boolean "engaged". A threshold baked into the client is a
threshold that cannot be revisited once the data is in, and this exists
precisely because we do not know where the line is.

Driven through a real Chromium, which was worth doing — the first version lost
two of the three cases it was built to catch. It waited for a 15-second timer
whose own condition excluded the signal the timer measures, and otherwise for
an unload beacon that a closing tab does not reliably deliver. So somebody who
stayed sixteen seconds without scrolling produced nothing, and somebody who
clicked and left after three seconds produced nothing. Both are exactly the
readers this is meant to distinguish from a crawler. It now sends the moment
any one signal lands, and `reason` records which:

| Simulated visit | `page_engaged` | first signal |
|---|---|---|
| Render and leave after 1.5s (crawler-like) | **0** | — |
| Stayed 16s, no scroll, no click | 1 | `dwell` |
| Clicked, left after 3s | 1 | `interaction` |
| Scrolled to 62% | 1 | `scroll` |
| Navigated to a second page | still 1 | no double-fire |

`dwell_seconds` is seconds-until-signal, **not** time on page — a lower bound
on attention, not a measure of it. Named `avg_seconds_to_signal` in the metrics
payload so nobody averages it as dwell time.

The same round closes the server-side labelling gap from §2: `botProps()` is
stamped on the calendar, feed, embed and llms events. Mobile checked at 390px:
zero horizontal overflow on the new page.

- **Measures:** `audience.engagement.rate_of_human_views`, `by_first_signal`,
  `scrolled_past_half`, `clicked_or_typed`; `audience.unclassified` falling.
- **Baseline:** unmeasurable — 7 "human" page views with no behavioural
  evidence attached to any of them; 149 unclassified production events.
- **Target (7 days):** every new server-recorded event classified. And the
  decisive one: **if ≥ 3 distinct human visitors produce a `page_engaged` in
  seven days, the traffic is real and the constraint is the site. If zero do,
  it is not, and the next month goes into getting anybody here at all.**
