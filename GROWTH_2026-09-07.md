Five changes against the three standing goals: revenue (£10k/month), trust,
and traffic. What shipped, why, what measures it, and how to run the review.

The previous round (2026-09-03) built the instrument. This round is the first
one that could read it — and what it says changed the plan.

## What the numbers actually said

`/api/admin/daily-metrics`, 2026-09-07. The funnel is instrumented
(`instrumented: true`), so these are measurements, not absences.

| Metric | 7d | 30d |
|---|---|---|
| Unique visitors | 14 | 14 |
| Page views | 20 | 20 |
| `register_started` / `register_completed` | 0 / 0 | — / 0 |
| `launch_subscribed` | 0 | 0 |
| `tool_started` | 0 | 0 |
| `article_cta_click` | 0 | — |

The 7d and 30d windows are identical because instrumentation only went live on
2026-09-03. Read these as "four days", not "a month".

Standing totals: 45 signups, 15 HMRC connections (33%), 1 bank connection,
0 filings, £0 MRR. Signups in the last 7 days: **0**.

Three findings, and they are what the whole round is built on.

**1. Traffic, not conversion, is the binding constraint.** Of 14 visitors,
**12 were direct** and exactly **two came from a search engine** — one from
google.co.uk, one from bing.com. The site publishes 109 articles, 3 free
calculators, 3 software landing pages and 9 competitor comparison pages. That
entire surface earned two organic visits in a week.

**2. The site does not link to itself.** The metrics endpoint lists **18 pages
with zero traffic**, and the reason was structural rather than editorial:
twenty pages each carried their own hand-written footer, and every one of them
linked Privacy and Terms and nothing else. A comparison page had **two**
internal links on it. Crawlers find pages by following links, and a page the
site itself never links to is one a search engine has little reason to keep in
its index. The `/tools` hub — built last round specifically to group the
calculators — had **0 views in 30 days**.

**3. The one asset that compounds pre-approval was collected on four pages,
none of which get traffic.** The launch waitlist stood at **0 subscribers**.
It existed only on `/`, `/pricing`, `/trust` and `/tools`, at roughly the
ninth section of ten — and on none of the 109 articles, which is where search
traffic actually lands.

There is also a plain bug behind finding 2: the header's "How it Works" link
pointed at `/#features`, an anchor that **has never existed** on the homepage
(the sections are `services`, `how`, `faq`, `notify`). It was a no-op in the
nav of every page on the site.

The conclusion I drew, and the reason this round contains no new landing page:
**building page 131 would repeat the mistake.** At two organic visits a week,
conversion-rate work is also unmeasurable — a "conversion rate" over 8
homepage visitors is noise. So everything below either makes the 130 pages we
already have discoverable, or captures an address from the trickle that does
arrive.

## What shipped

### F6 — One site-wide footer, with real internal linking
`components/SiteFooter.tsx` · 19 pages converted · 7 pages that never had a footer

Replaces twenty hand-rolled Privacy/Terms footers with a single component
linking the product pages, all three calculators and the tools hub, the guides
and topic hubs, and all nine comparison pages. It also carries the compact
launch capture, so every public page now has a low-commitment ask.

Measured effect on the rendered HTML (server-side, so crawler-visible):

| Page | Unique internal links before | After |
|---|---|---|
| `/xero-alternative` | 2 | 29 |
| An article page | ~5 | 33 |
| `/privacy` | 1 | 26 |

- **Measures:** `funnel.attribution.*.pages_with_no_traffic` (length);
  `launch_list.by_source["footer"]`.
- **Baseline:** 18 pages with zero traffic.
- **Target:** ≤ 10 within 14 days; ≤ 5 within 30 days.

### F7 — The dead nav anchor
`components/SiteHeader.tsx`

`/#features` → `/#how`, desktop and mobile. The homepage footer's `href="#"`
Twitter link is gone with the old footer rather than being re-pointed at a
profile we do not have.

- **Measures:** `/` page views vs `#how`-section engagement; no dead internal
  anchors in the rendered HTML.
- **Target:** zero `href="#"` and zero anchors with no matching `id` on any
  public page. Already true as of this commit — this one is verified, not
  forecast.

### F8 — Email capture where search traffic actually lands
`app/tax-tips/_components/ArticleCta.tsx` · `components/NotifyMeForm.tsx`

A compact variant of the waitlist form (no segment select, one row) now sits
at the foot of every article, inside the existing CTA block. The account CTA
stays, but it is the wrong size of ask for a stranger from a search result
when filing is not open yet; the address is what survives until approval.

- **Measures:** `launch_list.total`, `launch_list.by_source` — new this round,
  so `article` and `footer` are separable from the original page blocks.
- **Baseline:** 0 subscribers, 4 pages carrying the form.
- **Target:** ≥ 8 subscribers in 14 days, with ≥ half attributed to `article`
  or `footer`. Deliberately below the 25-in-14-days target set on 2026-09-03,
  which assumed traffic we do not have.

### F9 — The free tools, made reachable
`lib/tools.ts` · `components/ToolCrossLinks.tsx` · homepage strip · 3 tool pages

The tool list moves out of the hub page into `lib/tools.ts`, so the hub is no
longer the only page that knows the set exists. Each calculator now
cross-links the other two, and the homepage gets a tools section above the FAQ.
The tools are the only thing the product can give a stranger before HMRC
approval lands, so they should not sit behind a nav item.

- **Measures:** `tools.last_7d.hub_views`; `by_tool[].page_views`;
  `by_tool[].start_rate`.
- **Baseline:** hub 0 views/30d; 3 tools, 2 page views between them; 0 starts.
- **Target:** ≥ 20 tool page views/week and a `start_rate` ≥ 25% on any tool
  with a non-zero denominator.

### F10 — IndexNow
`lib/indexnow.ts` · `app/api/cron/indexnow/route.ts` · `public/<key>.txt` · `vercel.json`

A daily cron (08:30, just after the daily-article job) submits every sitemap
URL to IndexNow, which pushes changes to Bing, Yandex, Seznam and Naver in
hours instead of waiting for passive rediscovery. The URL list is derived from
`app/sitemap.ts` itself, so the two cannot drift — we never announce a page we
do not claim exists. A dry run resolves **156 URLs**.

Two honest caveats. **Google does not participate in IndexNow**, so this does
nothing for the larger of our two search referrers; the daily-article cron
remains the thing that gives Google a reason to return. And crawling is not
ranking — this removes a discovery bottleneck, it does not create demand.

- **Measures:** cron response `submitted` / `status`;
  `funnel.attribution.last_7d.by_channel` for `bing.com`.
- **Baseline:** 1 Bing visitor / 7d; 130+ pages of unknown index status.
- **Target:** submission returns 200/202 with ≥ 150 URLs; search-referred
  visitors 2/week → ≥ 10/week within 30 days.

## Deploy notes

No migration this round. `launch_subscribers.source` already existed and was
already being written — the metrics endpoint simply was not reading it. The
`by_source` block appears as soon as the code deploys.

One thing to confirm on production after deploy: IndexNow validates by
fetching `https://easytax.vip/55251a02303938bd113be2863f9c7b6c.txt`. That file
must be publicly reachable or every submission returns 403. The key is not a
secret — that is how the protocol proves host control.

```
curl -s https://easytax.vip/55251a02303938bd113be2863f9c7b6c.txt
curl -s "https://easytax.vip/api/cron/indexnow?dry=1&secret=$CRON_SECRET"
```

## The review — 2026-09-14 (7 days) and 2026-10-07 (30 days)

| # | Metric | Baseline | 7-day | 30-day |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| F6 | `pages_with_no_traffic` (count) | 18 | ≤ 12 | ≤ 5 |
| F7 | dead anchors on public pages | 1 | 0 | 0 |
| F8 | `launch_list.total` | 0 | ≥ 4 | ≥ 20 |
| F9 | tool page views / week | 2 | ≥ 10 | ≥ 20 |
| F10 | search-referred visitors / week | 2 | ≥ 5 | ≥ 10 |

**Read these as directions, not verdicts.** At 14 visitors a week every ratio
here has a single-digit denominator, and F6 and F10 act on crawler behaviour,
which moves on a scale of weeks. The 7-day column is a check that nothing
broke; the 30-day column is the actual test.

The one number that would falsify this round's whole thesis: if
`pages_with_no_traffic` is still 18 at 30 days *and* search-referred visitors
are still ~2/week, then discovery was not the constraint, demand was — and the
next round has to be about distribution off-site rather than anything in this
repository.
