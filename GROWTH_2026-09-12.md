# Growth work — 2026-09-12

Five changes against the three standing goals: traffic (the priority), trust
and revenue. What shipped, what measures it, and how to judge it.

Two things this round found before it built anything, and both are worse than
the numbers they were hiding.

---

## 1. The archive has been frozen for five days, and the freeze was self-inflicted

The 2026-09-06 round added a review gate: the daily cron stopped publishing
model-drafted tax guidance straight to the public archive and started writing
`review_status: 'draft'` instead, for a person to release. That was the right
call for 113 pages of YMYL content.

What was never built was the other half. The release action exists only as a
`POST /api/admin/article-review`, which in practice means a curl command with a
secret in it. So nothing was ever released:

| | |
|---|---|
| Last article to reach the public archive | **2026-09-07** |
| Drafts written since, none visible to any reader | 2 (09-08, 09-11) |
| Days the only traffic-earning part of the site has been frozen | **5** |

The cron itself is healthy — it has run at 08:19 every morning since 2026-08-11,
missing only 09-09 and 09-10. It has been writing into a queue nobody could
empty.

Two daily agent runs read `/api/admin/daily-metrics` in that window and neither
caught it, because the endpoint reported `drafts_awaiting_review: 2` — which
reads as a small healthy queue — and carried no number anywhere saying the
archive had stopped moving. **A count of what is waiting is not a measure of
whether anything is getting through.**

This is the 2026-09-11 lesson in a third register. That round was about not
knowing what was deployed. This one is about not knowing what was published.
Both are the same failure: a control was added, and the number that would show
the control misfiring was not.

## 2. Two of the three article cards on the homepage were dead links

Found while wiring up F29, and it follows directly from §1. When the review gate
landed, every reader of `tax_articles` was taught to ask for published rows
only — the article page, the sitemap, the feeds, the topic hubs. `app/page.tsx`
was not. It orders by `published_at` and takes three.

Drafts carry a `published_at`. So from 2026-09-08 the homepage led with the two
newest **drafts**, both linking to `/tax-tips/<slug>`, which 404s for anything
unreviewed:

```
 404 -> [draft]     2026-09-11  HMRC signed you up for Making Tax Digital — what happens now
 404 -> [draft]     2026-09-08  VAT Threshold Planning: Register Early or Wait for £90,000?
   ok   [published] 2026-09-07  Cash Basis & MTD ITSA: Invoice Date vs Payment Date Explained
```

The homepage is 39% of all production page views and the only surface any human
visitor currently touches. Two thirds of its content block sent them to a 404.

## 3. The archive answers 3 of the 41 questions it was built to answer

`lib/search-queries.ts` has carried a demand model since 2026-09-08: 41 queries
as a worried person would type them, each with a priority. Run the project's own
`coverage()` over the 113 published articles:

**41 target queries · 3 covered · 38 pending.**

Every one of the top eight uncovered queries is priority 1 and in the `mtd-live`
or `mtd-penalties` cluster — the timely questions that are the only edge a small
site has. The single highest-priority query on the list,
*"hmrc signed me up for making tax digital automatically"*, is answered by the
11,718-character, four-source draft that has been sitting in the review queue
since 09-11.

At one article a day, 38 queries is 38 days — and only once the queue can be
emptied at all.

Bot labelling (F24) went live in production at **2026-09-11T07:16**. Of the 13

### The one channel with a pulse

While search sent nothing for three days, the AI crawlers were busy. Over 48

That is not vanity traffic. For a pre-revenue site with 113 pages and no

## What shipped

Four of the five are traffic, which is the standing 80/20 split.

### F26 · A review queue a person can actually operate — `/admin/review`

*Goal: traffic.* The missing half of the 2026-09-06 gate. Lists every waiting
draft with how long it has waited, grades each against `STANDARD` as a row of
pass/fail chips (words, headings, gov.uk sources, figures, table, excerpt
length) so a review is a thirty-second decision rather than a twenty-minute
read, shows the full body and sources on request, and publishes or rejects in
one click through the existing API — which already stamps the reviewer, sets
`reviewed_at` and pings IndexNow.

The page leads with the number whose absence caused §1: **days since last
publish**, in red at 2 or more.

Access is the same `AGENT_METRICS_KEY` as `/api/admin/*`, checked server-side
before any content renders, with the page `noindex` and `/admin/` disallowed in
robots.txt. Deliberately not wired through `auth.ts`: that file is under active
HMRC review and an internal tool is not a reason to touch it.

- **Metric** `editorial.days_since_last_publish` · baseline **5** · target **≤2**
- **Also now reported** `oldest_draft_age_hours`, `last_published_at`,
  `pending_upgrades`

### F27 · `/llms.txt` and `/llms-full.txt`

*Goal: traffic.* The index an answer engine reads instead of crawling the nav:
the site summary, eight key pages with a line each on what they answer, the
topic hubs with article counts, all 113 articles with excerpts, and a citation
note. `/llms-full.txt` carries the complete text of every published article in
one file — 511KB, 113 articles, HTML converted to clean plain text.

The 16 named AI agents are now explicitly allowed in `robots.txt` rather than
left to the `*` rule, so the permission is deliberate and revocable per agent.
Fetches are recorded server-side as `llms_fetched` with the crawler's
user-agent, and are absent from `/api/track`'s allowlist on purpose: evidence a
browser can forge is not evidence.

Honest caveat: `llms.txt` is a convention, not a standard. A zero here means the
crawlers ignored it, not that it broke.

- **Metric** `distribution.last_7d.llms.distinct_agents` · baseline **0** · target **≥3 in 7 days**
- **Also reported** `full_text_fetches` separately — the index being fetched is
  interest; the full text being fetched is the archive leaving with someone.

### F28 · Section anchors, a contents list, and FAQPage structured data

*Goal: traffic and trust.* Every `<h2>`/`<h3>` in an article body now carries a
slug `id`, so a section of a 4,300-character page has a URL — for Google's jump
links, and for an answer engine citing one paragraph rather than the whole page.
Articles with four or more sections gain an "On this page" list. Applied at
render, so all 113 existing pages get it without a backfill.

FAQPage JSON-LD is emitted **only where the headings are genuinely questions**,
tested as ending in a question mark. That is 146 headings across **41 of the 113
articles**. The looser test — headings merely starting with "what" or "why" —
would have qualified 105 articles by sweeping in "Why the Timing of Income
Matters More Than Ever" and "What HMRC Typically Accepts", which are section
titles, not questions. Marking those up is what Google's structured-data policy
is about, and a manual action is a real cost to a site whose whole pitch is
being trustworthy about tax. A third of the archive marked up honestly beats all
of it marked up wrongly.

Verified against the real corpus: 113 articles, 0 promised-but-missing ids, 0
duplicate ids within a document, 0 unsafe id characters, 0 articles whose body
text changed, 41 emitting FAQPage over 105 Q&A pairs.

- **Metric** unique visitors/day to `/tax-tips*` · baseline **0.89** · target **2**
- Judge at 4–8 weeks, not at 7 days. Structured data does not move rankings in a
  week.

### F29 · The homepage routes by demand, and stops linking to 404s

*Goal: traffic.* Two parts.

The bug in §2 is fixed: the homepage article query now goes through
`selectPublished` like every other reader of that table.

And a new block surfaces the archive **ordered by demand rather than by date**,
built from the `TARGET_QUERIES` model that nothing outside the article cron has
ever read. The link text is the query as somebody would type it — *"Can i appeal
a HMRC late filing penalty reasonable excuse"* — with the article's real
headline underneath, so nothing is promised that the page does not deliver. Only
queries an article genuinely covers are shown, using the same `coversQuery` test
the cron uses to decide what to write next.

It renders three cards today, because three is what the archive earns (§3). The
block is therefore also a coverage gauge: it grows on its own as the pipeline
closes queries, and **if it is still three cards in a fortnight, that is the
finding.**

- **Metric** human page views/day, bot-filtered · baseline **5** · target **8**
- **Secondary** the card count itself, as archive coverage of the demand model

### F30 · The numbers that would have caught all of this

*Goal: trust.* `editorial` in `/api/admin/daily-metrics` now carries
`days_since_last_publish`, `last_published_at`, `oldest_draft_age_hours` and
`pending_upgrades` alongside the draft count, read through a single
`lib/review-queue.ts` shared with `/admin/review` so the page and the metric
cannot drift apart. `distribution` gains an `llms` block.

`lib/growth-targets.ts` gains F26–F30, so the weekly review stays arithmetic
rather than recollection. F26's verdict is inverted — it passes by going down —
and F30's target is set at the observed baseline (0.38 human share) rather than
above it: the point of that one is that the split stays in front of the review,
not that it gets beaten.

- **Metric** `audience.last_7d.human_share` · baseline **0.38** · reported, not chased

## What to do with this

**One action is worth more than the other four put together:** open
`/admin/review?key=…` and publish the 2026-09-11 draft. It is the longest,
best-sourced article in the archive, and it answers the single highest-priority
query in the demand model — the one HMRC is creating new searchers for every day
it auto-enrols another tranche of the £50,000 cohort. It has been invisible for
a day; the 09-08 draft has been invisible for four.

Then decide the standing question this round could not decide alone: whether a
draft that passes every `STANDARD` check and cites three or more gov.uk sources
should auto-publish after some number of hours if no person has looked at it.
That would trade some editorial control for a pipeline that cannot silently
stop. It is a judgement about how much review these pages need, and it belongs
to the person whose name is on them — which is why this round built the queue
rather than quietly removing the gate.
