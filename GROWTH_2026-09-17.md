Five changes against the three standing goals: traffic (the priority), trust

how to judge it.

## 1. The archive cannibalised itself

This is the finding of the round.

A `site:easytax.vip` search returns **one page — the homepage**. Not one of the
113 published articles, and not one of the 9 competitor comparison pages,
surfaces at all. Against that, search sent 6 visitors in 7 days.

The reason is in the archive. Clustering all 113 published headlines by content-
word similarity:

| Subject | Times written |
|---|---|
| Trading allowance | 5 |
| Capital allowances on plant & machinery | 5 |
| Home office expenses | 5 |
| MTD ITSA quarterly updates | 4 |
| VAT partial exemption | 3 — two under a **byte-identical** headline |

**Thirty-three of 113 articles are a near-duplicate of another one.** Five pages
on one low-authority domain answering "trading allowance vs actual expenses" is
not five chances to rank. It is one chance split five ways, on a domain that has
to spend its authority choosing between its own pages before it can spend any of
it competing with anybody else's.

What makes this a genuine miss rather than a known cost: the project already
*knew*. `findTitleCollision`'s own header comment names the VAT partial
exemption pair and the five Capital Allowances pages, and has refused to
commission a colliding headline since 2026-09-14. `reconcileQueue` (F46) clears
duplicates out of the review queue. **Both operate on drafts.** Nothing has ever
been pointed at the 113 rows already published, because the checks were built
after the archive had already accumulated its duplicates.

## 2. The nine pages with the most buying intent had no parent

Every `/*-alternative` page — QuickBooks, Xero, FreeAgent, Sage, Crunch,
Coconut, KashFlow, Bokio, TaxScouts — had **zero page views over 7 days and zero
over 30**. They are not thin and they are not duplicates: 1,800–2,700 words
each, own feature tables, own pricing arithmetic. Somebody searching "Xero
alternative UK" is shopping, not reading.

Their entire internal linking was a row of nine bare product names in the site
footer. `/mtd-software` — 2,900 words, the obvious topical parent of "which MTD
software should I use" — linked to **none** of them. So the nine pages that
answer a buying question had no path from any page that ranks, on a site where
the only page search surfaces is the homepage.

## 3. A migration shipped yesterday and never ran

`tax_articles.target_query` does not exist in production. F47 — articles that
state the search they were commissioned to answer — went live on 2026-09-16
with `supabase/migrations/20260916_article_target_query.sql`, and the migration
was never applied. `insertDraft` hit the missing column, retried without it
exactly as designed, logged a warning nobody reads, and the feature did nothing
for a day.

Nothing broke, which is the point. Every reader of this schema is deliberately
fail-soft, so a missing column degrades silently *by construction*. It was found
this morning by accident, because a query asking for the column came back
`42703`.

That is the sixth time in eleven rounds that a control existed and the number
that would have shown it misfiring did not.

## What shipped

### F51 — Consolidate the archive onto one page per subject
`lib/article-clusters.ts` · `lib/article-canonical.ts` · `app/tax-tips/[slug]`
· `app/sitemap.ts` · feeds · `lib/llms.ts`

Published articles are clustered by headline similarity (Jaccard over content
words, union-find, threshold 0.7). Each cluster elects the most recently
published page as primary; the rest get a canonical tag pointing at it and drop
out of the sitemap, both feeds and llms.txt.

**Nothing is deleted, unpublished or rewritten.** Every URL still resolves and
still renders its full article, because somebody may hold the link. Only the
indexing signal changes, which is what makes this reversible — deleting the
pages would not be.

The threshold was calibrated against the live archive, not picked. At 0.7 the
six clusters it finds are the six that every reading of the list agrees on. At
0.65 it starts pulling in pairs like "Subsistence Costs for Sole Traders"
against "Meals and Entertainment: What Sole Traders Can Actually Claim" — which
overlap, but which of two angles should survive is an editorial judgement and
not an agent's to make silently. Those 21 pairs are reported as
`review_candidates` and left alone.

- **Measures:** `archive_duplication.{clusters, canonicalised, indexable}`;
  sitemap URL count; search-referred visitors/week.
- **Baseline:** 113 published, 33 involved in a near-duplicate pair, 156 sitemap
  URLs, 6 search visitors/week.
- **Target:** 105 indexable articles, 0 duplicate titles in the SEO audit.
  Verified against a local production build on live data: 6 clusters, 8 pages
  canonicalised, sitemap 156 → 149 (−8 duplicates, +1 for `/compare`), no
  canonical chains, no primary wrongly canonicalised, and an unrelated article
  untouched and self-canonical.

### F52 — Stop the pipeline writing the same page a sixth time
`lib/article-quality.ts`

`findTitleCollision` gains a similarity test on the same threshold F51
consolidates on. `exact` and `stem` are both equality tests on a fixed slice of
the headline, so one word inside the stem defeats them — "Capital Allowances
**for** Plant & Machinery" against "Capital Allowances **on** Plant & Machinery",
five weeks apart, both published — and a headline with no colon has the whole
title as its stem, so one synonym defeats it: "Allowable Expenses UK Freelancers
**Miss**" against "…**Forget**", published the same day.

**Honest scope.** Replaying all 113 published articles in publication order
through the upgraded function: `stem` refuses 16 and is doing most of the work;
the new test refuses 2 more that it structurally cannot see. This is a
gap-filler on a check that mostly works, not a replacement for it. The archive
that would exist had this been in force from the start is 94 distinct pages
rather than 113.

- **Measures:** near-duplicate pairs created after 2026-09-17;
  `archive_duplication.clusters` over time.
- **Baseline:** 6 clusters, growing by roughly one a fortnight.
- **Target:** no new cluster ever. Verified by the replay above.

### F53 — Report the migrations that have not run
`lib/schema-state.ts` · `/api/admin/daily-metrics`

A probe per migration for the one object it adds, reported as
`schema.pending`. It reads and never writes: applying DDL is the one operation
here where an agent guessing wrong is not cheaply reversible.

One defect was found and fixed in this during verification, and it is the same
mistake the module exists to catch. The obvious implementation asks PostgREST
for the column with `head: true`, transferring no rows. A HEAD response has no
body by definition, so when PostgREST rejects the column it reports
`error=42703` in a header and returns nothing for supabase-js to parse — which
hands back an error with an empty message and no `code`. The first version
therefore reported the one genuinely missing migration as `inconclusive: ""`:
a probe built to make a silent failure visible, failing silently. It asks for
one row now.

- **Measures:** `schema.pending`.
- **Baseline:** `["20260916_article_target_query"]` — one migration shipped
  2026-09-16 and still not applied.
- **Target:** empty. Verified against the live database.

### F54 — Give the answer engines facts, de-duplicated
`lib/llms.ts` · `app/llms.txt`

Two changes to the surfaces the crawlers actually read. The article index and
both feeds now list each subject once rather than handing GPTBot the trading
allowance five times — they over-fetch and then de-duplicate, so the feed still
carries a full 50 items and simply reaches deeper into the archive.

And `llms.txt` opens with a **Key facts** block: the mandate sentence, the
qualifying-income definition, all three thresholds, the four quarterly deadlines
with the Final Declaration, and the penalty regime for the current year. A model
fetching this file is usually answering one question and for this site that
question is almost always a date or a threshold, so the answer goes above the
page index.

Every figure is computed from `lib/mtd-dates.ts`, `lib/mtd-status.ts` and
`lib/mtd-penalties.ts` — the same functions the deadline checker and the
timetable render from. Nothing there is a second copy that could drift from the
page, which is the property that makes it safe to invite a model to quote it.

- **Measures:** `distribution.llms.fetches`, `oai-searchbot` fetches, duplicate
  entries in the feeds.
- **Baseline:** 1 llms.txt fetch/week; feeds carrying up to 5 copies of a
  subject.
- **Target:** ≥ 4 llms.txt fetches/week; 0 duplicate entries. Verified against a
  production build: the facts block renders with live computed dates (next
  update Q2 2026/27, due 7 November 2026), and `vat-partial-exemption` appears
  exactly once in llms.txt and once in each feed.

### F55 — Give the comparison pages an internal home
`lib/competitors.ts` · `app/compare` · `app/mtd-software` · `app/page.tsx` ·
`components/SiteFooter.tsx` · 7 locale files

A `/compare` hub listing all nine with price, who each product is genuinely
for, and one reason somebody might leave it; linked from the homepage, from
`/mtd-software`, and from the footer heading that previously labelled nine bare
links. The nine paths now come from one list — the footer used to keep its own
second copy, so a tenth comparison page would have had to be added in two files
and would have been added to one.

The copy rule is a trust rule, not a style one: say what the other product is
better at before saying why somebody might leave. A reader on that page is
currently paying one of these companies.

**One thing was caught before it shipped.** The first draft of
`lib/competitors.ts` carried prices from memory — QuickBooks at £10/mo,
TaxScouts at £169. The pages say £14 and £119–169. Every figure was re-read off
the page it links to. Publishing a competitor's price wrongly on a tax product's
own site is the cheapest possible way to lose a reader who knows exactly what
they currently pay.

- **Measures:** `compare_hub_viewed`, `compare_cta_click`, page views on the
  nine `/*-alternative` paths.
- **Baseline:** 0 views on all nine, over both 7 and 30 days.
- **Target:** > 0 within 7 days. Verified: all nine render on `/compare` with
  canonical and `ItemList` JSON-LD, `/mtd-software` now carries all nine plus
  the hub, and the homepage strip resolves in all seven locales.

`npx tsc --noEmit` and `npm run build` pass. Every claim above was checked
against a local production server on live Supabase data rather than reasoned
about — the canonical tags on four specific URLs, the sitemap arithmetic, the
feed contents, the facts block, and both new metrics blocks.

Nothing here touches `lib/hmrc*.ts`, `app/api/hmrc/**`, `auth.ts`,
`middleware.ts`, `DeviceDataCollector.tsx` or `app/api/auth/callback/hmrc/`.

**`supabase/migrations/20260916_article_target_query.sql` still needs to be
run.** It is yesterday's and it is still pending. `schema.pending` now says so
on every metrics fetch instead of nobody noticing for a day.

Two deliberate non-changes, both recorded so the next round does not treat them
as oversights:

- **`/tax-tips` and `/tax-tips/page/N` still list the whole archive**, including
  the duplicates. Those listings are what give older articles an inbound
  internal link, and shortening them would orphan pages to fix a problem the
  canonical tag has already fixed.
- **The homepage was examined and left alone.** It takes 47% of traffic and
  `by_path` shows 0 conversions against it, which looks alarming and is an
  attribution artifact: `register_started` 3 → `register_completed` 3 is a 100%
  completion rate in the same window. Prior rounds have already tracked its
  CTAs, surfaced the tools and added the calendar. The constraint is that
  nobody arrives, not that arrivals bounce.

Still outstanding, none of them mine to decide:

- **One draft is waiting, 193 hours old**, and the archive has published
  nothing for 10 days. It is one real decision now rather than three, which is
  F46 working.
- **The reactivation campaign is built and unsent**, seven rounds running. It
  mails 45 real people who have never received anything from us. Outward-facing
  and irreversible, and still the strongest revenue lever before approval.

F51, F52, F53 and F54 are readable at a sample size of one — none depends on

**The number that would falsify this round:** *pages surfacing for
`site:easytax.vip`* at 30 days. The whole thesis is that 113 articles draw six
search visitors because the archive competes with itself and the good pages have
no internal path. If on 2026-10-17 the duplicates are consolidated, the
comparison pages are linked from three places, the feeds are clean — and Google
still shows exactly one page — then cannibalisation was not the constraint, this
site simply has no authority yet, and no further on-page work will buy any. The
next round would then belong to off-site: HMRC's published list of MTD-compatible
software, which is free, authoritative, aimed precisely at our customer, and
gated on the approval that is already pending.
