# Growth work — 2026-09-06

Five changes against the three standing goals: revenue (£10k/month), trust,
traffic. What shipped, why, how each is measured, and what the numbers have to
say before any of it counts.

---

## Where the numbers are

From `/api/admin/daily-metrics` on 2026-09-06.

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Unique visitors, 7d | **9** |
| Page views, 7d | 12 |
| Signups, 7d / 30d | 0 / 3 |
| `register_started`, 7d | **0** |
| Launch-list subscribers | **0** (shipped 3 days ago) |
| Tool completions, 7d | 0 |
| Filings | 0 |
| MRR | £0 — HMRC production approval pending |

Search channels, 7 days: google.co.uk 1 visitor, bing.com 1. Everything else
direct. Pages with **zero** traffic in 30 days: `/pricing`, `/tools`, all three
calculators bar one, and all eight competitor-comparison pages.

Three readings, and they set the whole plan.

**1. The binding constraint is traffic, not conversion.** Nine visitors a week.
At that volume no conversion-rate change is measurable — a single extra click
moves the rate by eleven points. Anything reported as a percentage of nine is
noise, and this document treats it as such.

**2. Yesterday's five features produced no measurable change, and the reason is
instructive.** Three calculators, a tools hub, eight comparison pages and a
waitlist shipped on 3 September. In the seven days since, the hub got 0 views,
the comparison pages got 0, and the waitlist converted 0 of 9. Publishing more
pages into a site nobody reaches does not produce traffic. Repeating it would
have been the easy move and the wrong one.

**3. The archive is the asset, and it is built on sand.** 140 articles of UK tax
guidance — the most Your-Money-Your-Life category there is — generated by a
model on a cron and published the same second, with no author, no reviewer, no
sources and no stated method. Nobody read any of them before they went live.
That is the best available explanation for why 140 decent pages draw three page
views a week, and it is the one thing here that code can actually fix.

So: two changes aimed at making the archive eligible to rank, one at capturing
the visitors we do get with an offer that is deliverable today, one at making
the funnel honest, and one at being able to tell next week whether any of it
worked.

## What shipped

### F1 — Editorial accountability and a review gate
`supabase/migrations/20260906_*.sql` · `app/tax-tips/_lib/review.ts` ·
`app/tax-tips/_components/ArticleProvenance.tsx` · `app/editorial-standards` ·
`app/api/admin/article-review` · `app/api/cron/daily-article`

New articles now land as `draft` and are invisible to readers, to the sitemap
and to the topic hubs until a person publishes them. Publishing stamps the
reviewer and the date, which is what the notice on the page and the `reviewedBy`
in its JSON-LD are drawn from — neither can claim a review that did not happen.

The 140 existing articles stay published and each now carries an honest notice
saying it has **not** been individually checked. `/editorial-standards` says the
same thing in full: AI-drafted, from what, reviewed by whom, and which parts of
the archive nobody has read yet. Being straight about it is the trust play; a
page headed "our expert team" would have been worse than saying nothing.

The generator is also now asked to cite the HMRC manual or statute behind each
figure, and those render as a Sources block.

- **Measures:** `funnel.attribution.last_7d` unique visitors to `/tax-tips*`;
  `editorial.drafts_awaiting_review`; `editorial_standards_viewed`.
- **Baseline / target:** 3 → 12 unique visitors per week.
- **Honest horizon:** four to eight weeks. Week one is a leading indicator, not
  a verdict, and the scorecard says so rather than reporting a number as though
  it settled anything.
- **Risk, and how it is handled:** getting this wrong would unpublish 140
  indexed pages. Every reader goes through `selectPublished()`, which retries
  unfiltered if the column is absent. The local build with the migration
  unapplied logged the fallback and served the full archive — the deploy-before-
  migration path is exercised, not assumed.

### F2 — IndexNow instant indexing
`lib/indexnow.ts` · `public/755f…ed.txt` · `app/api/admin/indexnow` · `app/layout.tsx`

Bing is one of only two search referrers the site has. Bing, Yandex and Seznam
honour IndexNow; a ping moves discovery from "whenever a crawler next visits a
low-authority domain" to minutes. Publishing an article pings automatically;
`/api/admin/indexnow` submits the whole sitemap (dry run by default). Bing
verification is now an env var (`BING_SITE_VERIFICATION`) rather than a code
change.

Submissions are suppressed outside production — a preview deployment telling
search engines that easytax.vip changed is how a host gets its pings ignored.

- **Measures:** search-engine unique visitors, 7d; Bing Webmaster Tools indexed
  page count.
- **Baseline / target:** 2 → 8 per week.
- **Caveat:** Google does not support IndexNow. Judge this on Bing and Yandex.

### F3 — Deadline schedule by email, replacing the waitlist ask
`app/api/schedule` · `components/DeadlineScheduleForm.tsx` · on the checker,
`/tools`, `/trust` and every article

The waitlist asked for an address and offered, in exchange, a message at an
unknown future date about filing HMRC has not approved. It converted 0 of 9.

The new ask takes the same address and sends back, within the minute, the
visitor's own MTD dates: which tax year brings them in, four quarterly
deadlines, final declaration — computed from `lib/mtd-dates.ts`, the same source
the on-site checker uses. The dates also render on the page, so someone who
mistyped their address still gets what they came for. It carries a working
one-click unsubscribe and a `List-Unsubscribe` header (PECR reg. 22).

It sits at the highest-intent moment on the site: the instant someone finishes
the deadline checker, pre-filled with the income they just typed. That moment
was previously spent on a link to another page's waitlist.

- **Measures:** `funnel.last_7d.schedule_requested`, `schedule_sent`, by source.
- **Baseline / target:** 0 → 5 in 7 days.
- **Why it matters for revenue:** it is the only thing that compounds before
  approval — a list segmented by known filing obligation and date, warmed by
  useful mail, ready to convert on approval day.

### F4 — An honest funnel, and a stale statutory date fixed
`app/page.tsx` · `messages/*.json` (7 locales) · `app/trust/page.tsx` ·
`app/tax-tips/_components/ArticleCta.tsx`

`register_started` is 0. Every ask on the site was to create an account for
filing that cannot happen yet, and a third of visitors went to `/trust` — the
highest-engaged page — and left.

- The homepage announcement bar had a **hardcoded, wrong, and now past** date:
  "Q1 update due 5 Aug 2026". The statutory date is the 7th. It now comes from
  `nextQuarterDeadline()`, the same computed source as the hero pill, and states
  where HMRC approval stands.
- The trust bar's unverifiable claims ("Bank-grade Security", "File in 5
  Minutes") are replaced with facts a visitor can check: read-only bank access,
  ICO reference ZA540758, Finance Panda Limited. All seven locales updated.
- `/trust` and the article CTA now end in something deliverable today rather
  than in a register button that leads nowhere useful.

- **Measures:** share of unique visitors who give an email address, 7d;
  `trust_viewed` → capture; `register_started`.
- **Baseline / target:** 0% → 10%.
- **Sample-size gate:** 60 visitors in the window before this verdict means
  anything. It will almost certainly report "not enough data" next week, which
  is the correct answer, not a failure.

### F5 — Weekly scorecard with stored baselines
`lib/growth-targets.ts` · `app/api/admin/weekly-review` · `app/api/cron/growth-snapshot`

The targets above live in code, so next week's review is arithmetic rather than
recollection. A daily cron stores the whole metrics payload in
`growth_snapshots` (one row per day, upserted, so an at-least-once cron is
harmless); Mondays it emails the scorecard — every target with hit / missed /
not-enough-data, plus movement against the snapshot from a week earlier.

Each target carries the sample size it needs before its verdict counts. At nine
visitors a week that gate will fire often, and it should: a target that "passes"
on two conversions has told us nothing.

- **Measures:** `growth_snapshots.last_7d` ≥ 5; a review email that arrives.

## What this does not fix, and who has to

Code cannot manufacture distribution. Three things outside the repo now matter
more than anything in it:

1. **Google Search Console.** Nothing here tells us how many of the 169 URLs are
   actually indexed, or what they rank for. That number is the single most
   useful missing fact, and it takes one login.
2. **Backlinks.** A domain with no external links does not rank for commercial
   terms, which is why eight competitor-comparison pages drew zero visits in a
   month. They will keep drawing zero until something links to the site.
3. **Pricing.** £24 per submission is not recurring revenue. £10,000/month at
   that price is roughly 417 submissions a month. Whether the goal is
   reachable at all depends on a pricing decision that is the owner's to make,
   not one to slip into a deploy.

## Deploying

1. Run `supabase/migrations/20260906_editorial_and_scorecard.sql` in the
   Supabase SQL editor. Safe against the live build: additive only, and every
   default preserves current behaviour.
2. Confirm `https://easytax.vip/755f842c2f5b70b46ea93245998017ed.txt` returns
   the key, then `GET /api/admin/indexnow?key=…&submit=1` once.
3. Set `BING_SITE_VERIFICATION` from Bing Webmaster Tools.
4. `GET /api/admin/weekly-review?key=…&snapshot=1&label=baseline%202026-09-06`
   to record the baseline these targets are measured against.
